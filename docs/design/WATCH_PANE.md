# Watch Pane

A visual philosophy of two fields held in unequal time. One field is a witness: compact, vertical, almost still. The other is a stream: long, readable, allowed to travel. Between them a hairline, not a wall. Below them a bar of status that is allowed to arrive last and sit in front, like a terminal that never yields its last line.

Space is budgeted as a craftsman budgets timber: small units stacked into larger pauses. The witness column is a measured plank; the stream is the longer board. Negative space is not leftover. It is the joint. Form stays rectangular and honest. Corners soften only where a hand would rest—pills, portraits, controls—never as decoration on the architecture itself.

Color is a two-ink shop. Near-black field. One luminous green used as a signal, never as wallpaper. White for speech. A restrained yellow only when a thing is unfinished. Material is screen, not paper: matte planes, thin rules, no invented chrome. Every hue is mixed as if the printer would refuse a third pass.

Scale follows a quiet drum of eight. Rhythm is vertical: identity holds, content walks, footer stamps. Composition is left-weighted on purpose, like a bench visor that must not drift. Balance is not symmetry. Balance is a heavy small column against a long light one, calibrated until neither feels like a leftover.

Hierarchy is spatial before it is typographic. What stays is closer to the eye’s first rest. What moves is given a longer line. Type is sparse: names, verbs, measures. Words sit as labels on a diagram, not as essays on a poster. The work must look meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise, painstaking attention, master-level execution—hours spent aligning a rail to a bar until the last pixel of overlap is a decision, not an accident.
